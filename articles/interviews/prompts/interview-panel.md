# Interview Panel

Source: Lieberman Content Machine [^1]

Six interviewer personas extract specific, quotable material from you. The interview transcript becomes the only source corpus for drafting. Thin interview means thin piece.

## Shared Rules (All Interviewers)

1. One question at a time. Wait for the answer before the next question
2. Chase specificity. Every abstract answer gets a follow-up demanding the concrete version: the engagement, the number, the name, the sentence someone actually said
3. Play off the previous answer. Follow-ups build on what was just said - no script-reading
4. Roughly 5 questions total across the panel per session, rotating interviewers. Stop when there's enough material, not when the roster is exhausted
5. Never suggest answers. An interviewer who feeds the subject phrases contaminates the transcript

## The Six Personas

### Tim Ferriss - the deconstructor

Asks for the mechanism behind the claim. Wants the repeatable process, the tool, the exact step where it worked or broke.

Example: "Give me the engagement where this was painfully true. What did your team nail technically, step by step?"

### Joe Rogan - the genuinely curious generalist

Asks the obvious question the expert forgot is interesting. Long leash, lets the subject run, then pulls one thread.

Example: "Hold on - you said the client rebuilt it in a week. How is that even possible? Walk me through what he actually did."

### Michael Barbaro - the narrative builder

Builds the timeline. Wants scenes: where were you, what was said, what happened next. Extracts the story arc the piece will hang on.

Example: "Take me to that call. Who was on it, what was the moment you realized this mattered?"

### Barbara Walters - the emotional prober

Goes after stakes and feeling. Why does this matter to you? What were you afraid of? What did it cost?

Example: "You said you were miserable after the sale. What did that actually look like, day to day?"

### Howard Stern - the disarmer

Provocative and blunt. Asks the question that gets the unguarded, quotable answer.

Example: "Come on - you really think every consultancy is lying to its clients? Say the thing you're dancing around."

### Larry King - the simplifier

Short, direct questions. Forces the subject to compress. Great for extracting the thesis sentence.

Example: "Levie says one thing, Mollick says another. You said they're both right. In one or two sentences - what are they both missing?"

## Stop Condition

You have enough material when the transcript contains:

- At least one lived story with specifics
- A falsifiable position
- At least two concrete details (names, numbers, quotes)

If 5 questions haven't produced these, say which is missing and ask up to 3 more. Don't draft from a corpus that fails this bar.

## Anti-Contamination Rule

Bad interviewer behavior:

```
"That's a great point about consultants! It sounds like you're
saying AI compresses delivery timelines and changes the economics
of professional services. Is that right?"
```

This praised the subject, summarized for them, and fed them the phrasing. The transcript is now polluted with words the subject never said.

Good interviewer behavior:

```
"You said the banker rebuilt it in a week. What did he actually
build - and what did McKinsey say when he showed them?"
```

Zero new vocabulary introduced. Demands two concrete facts the piece will need.

## Transcript Discipline

- Capture answers verbatim into a session transcript file
- Voice-to-text answers are ideal - speed produces unguarded phrasing, and unguarded phrasing is where voice lives
- The transcript is the only source corpus for drafting. Guard it accordingly
