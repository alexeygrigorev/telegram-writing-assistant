# Million Dollar Weekend - Book Notes

I'm listening to Million Dollar Weekend. The author is talking about the Rule of 100, and it resonated with me. Commit to doing something 100 times before you stop. You don't have to become a professional, but by doing it repeatedly you'll learn enough to do it reasonably well.[^1]

## Repeating before giving up

The author used podcasts as an example. Many people want to start one, but they give up. When I started the DataTalks.Club podcast, I planned around 10 to 15 episodes in advance. That helped me keep going.[^1]

I've now done more than 200 episodes. I wouldn't necessarily call myself a professional, but it's easy for me to do. I kept going because I had a plan. I'd already agreed with guests and actively promoted the episodes, so backing out of that plan was difficult.[^1]

I think I can apply this to social media posts, newsletters, and other activities. I haven't reached 100 repetitions in many of them yet, but the more often I do them, the easier they get. It's the same with courses: every next course is much easier. I'll keep listening to hear what else the author says, but I wanted to record this note now.[^1]

## Making repetition easy

At first, you may do the work without seeing results. You need to repeat it before you get a response. When you start posting on social media, the first 10 or 20 posts may get no reaction. You have to post many times. One of them may eventually take off.[^2]

The author says you need a system that makes it easy to keep repeating the work. You have to continue even without the growth or response you want. Trust the process: results may come with time, but they won't come if you stop. If you stop at 50 instead of following through to 100, you haven't completed the commitment.[^2]

Understand the benefits, commit to 100 repetitions, and make doing the work easy enough that it doesn't take a huge effort each time. If every repetition takes a lot of effort and produces no result, of course you'll want to quit. That's why I want a system that makes continuing easy.[^2]

## Choosing one marketing goal

In the marketing chapter, the author says to choose a concrete goal and follow it. Every activity should move you toward that goal. If an activity doesn't, it's better to skip it.[^3]

I should set a goal for each product and ask whether each activity brings me closer to it. I don't have that right now. I do things quite ad hoc. Focusing on a goal would help me avoid getting distracted by other things.[^3]
