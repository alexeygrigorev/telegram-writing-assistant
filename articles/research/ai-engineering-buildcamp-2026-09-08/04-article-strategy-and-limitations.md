## 5. Article strategy, style and publication decisions

### Recommended angle

Lead with what people can inspect: projects, specific engineering decisions and the course’s learning progression. Use the new article to explain the common process behind the showcases, not to reproduce the September 4 list or present selected praise as a comprehensive independent verdict.

The draft uses three types of evidence visibly: a participant’s original learning note, attributed comments/testimonials with their hosting context, and student-owned project repositories. It discusses the redesign and finishing challenge without inventing an outcome guarantee. The critical database thread stays in the dossier; the article instead states realistic limits of capstones and deployment readiness.

### Observed blog style

The reference posts use direct first-person explanations, short paragraphs, practical examples, clear section headings and links to projects or supporting material. Long articles remain skimmable through topic-specific subheadings. Course promotion usually comes after something useful rather than being the only content. Technical detail is grounded in actual projects. ### Visual and file specifications used

The article contains three original conceptual illustrations: the course progression, four practice tracks and the evaluation feedback loop. They are exported at 1024 × 506, 1024 × 493 and 1024 × 516 pixels respectively. They retain landscape proportions, readable labels, white space and the soft palette observed in the course post. They are not screenshots or evidence of a running product.

The Word article uses a single 6.35-inch text/image column, serif body text, strong sans-serif headings and understated captions. The HTML preview uses a 680-pixel maximum reading column, responsive images at 100% column width, and no sidebar or corporate-report framing. The Markdown preserves placements, captions, alt text and links.

These are deliberate production dimensions, not claimed measurements of the website’s original files. ### Claims and wording to avoid

Do not say all 40 reviews were read; everyone finishes; the course produces a guaranteed job; 100,000 people took this paid course; every example is mandatory; all showcased projects are audited production systems; or a small tuning-set score is general accuracy. Do not cite LLM Zoomcamp testimonials as Buildcamp graduates, promote unauthorized redistribution, or turn a non-attendee’s criticism into a verified course-experience review.

The article’s project examples explicitly avoid clinical, legal, employment and operational-deployment guarantees. Its current-date call to action should be refreshed before publication, especially if publishing after September 21, 2026. The price and subscriber offer are linked to their source rather than presented as permanent terms. ## 6. Coverage and limitations

The research followed the course’s current and former names across public web search, Maven, the blog and affiliated site, LinkedIn, X, Reddit, Facebook, GitHub, learning roundups and surfaced redistribution/syndication pages. Searches included combinations of course title, instructor name, review, testimonial, scholarship, project and platform, followed by original-author checks and repository links.

The collection is broad but not the complete internet. Private communities, deleted posts, unindexed pages, private messages, paywalled review records and platform-only content were outside the accessible evidence. Dynamic Maven review bodies were not retrieved. Some Facebook, X and syndicated results remained snippet-only. The X detailed-review lead returned 403.

Dates are recorded only as precisely as the evidence supports. LinkedIn and Reddit relative ages were inconsistent between crawls; they were not converted into invented exact publication dates. Indexed dates are labeled as such. Sources in the register distinguish full-page/README inspection from indexed discovery.

The GitHub work was a documentary inspection, not running capstone code, verifying live uptime, validating privacy practices, auditing security, or reproducing evaluation results. Demos linked by authors are useful leads, but a public link alone is not proof of deployment reliability.

No representative sentiment score, review-frequency percentage, career-impact estimate, course completion rate or causal improvement claim can be derived from this collection. The usable conclusion is narrower: the public record shows substantive project work, several attributable positive learning signals, documented course iteration and real constraints that should be communicated clearly.
