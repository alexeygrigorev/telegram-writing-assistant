# AI-Assisted Automation Course Idea

After spending significant time with AI assistants for automation, an idea is forming for a new course focused on AI-assisted automation. This would complement existing courses like AI DevTools Zoomcamp and ML Zoomcamp[^1].

## Course Concept

While the initial thought was "AI-assisted development," the focus is shifting toward "AI-assisted automation" - using AI to automate tasks including many aspects of development work[^1].

## Tool Preferences

For coding tasks specifically, Copilot's code output is preferred and more convenient to use. However, Claude Code excels at practically everything else - general automation tasks that go beyond just writing code[^1].

## Timeline

In a couple of months, there should be enough material to create a comprehensive course. The plan is to ask the audience about their interest level before committing to full development[^1].

## Course Format

If created, the course would likely be:
- Paid (complementing existing free content)
- Some topics offered as free webinars
- Structured knowledge building on AI DevTools Zoomcamp foundation[^1]

## Why Paid Courses

There are multiple reasons for preferring paid courses:

## Financial Reasons
- Additional income source - revenue from DataTalks Club isn't always sufficient
- Monetizing expertise and experience directly[^2]

## Audience Benefits
- AI DevTools Zoomcamp already provides free content with substantial value
- People who follow the link already learn a lot
- Paid course allows structuring knowledge more comprehensively[^2]

## Personal Motivation
- AI Buildcamp success demonstrates people are willing to pay for this expertise
- Students in paid courses tend to be more engaged
- Satisfaction from earning directly from knowledge and experience[^2]

This is a brainstorm/braindump format - some ideas may be developed further, others may not be suitable for publishing[^2].

## Target Audience: Business People, Not Engineers

Hugo pointed out that engineers are not the highest-paying audience. They typically do not control budgets. Hamel Husain's course targets PMs and product managers who can spend more on courses because they manage budgets directly[^3].

The current course is designed for engineers. A better focus might be business people who need to automate their processes. They have more money and can spend it on courses[^3].

This aligns with real experience. DataTalks.Club runs on automated business processes. The entire business was built from scratch with documented, automated workflows. That is practical knowledge to share: how to automate business processes with AI, using Codex, Claude Code, and similar tools[^3].

A recent interview reinforced this direction. The interviewer's company automates business processes for clients. The course could target more technically-minded founders who want to figure this out and do it themselves rather than hiring an agency. The course would walk them through everything in detail[^3].

The course could be small but priced high. It is worth trying on Maven to see how people react. The existing audience is not the ideal match, but Maven might surface business people who are looking for exactly this kind of course[^3].

This could also be a path to consulting: helping business people automate their processes one on one after they go through the course material[^3].

## Systemology Book: Course Content Ideas

Reading the Systemology book surfaced several ideas for the course[^4][^5][^6].

### Starting Angle: Stepping Away from the Business

The book opens with the author finding an opportunity and being able to step away from his business to pursue it. This is also a compelling angle for the course. It means being able to step away - take a vacation with a son, take time for yourself. Living in Europe, in Germany, it is possible to drop to about 10% of usual availability for two months without losing much traction. The business keeps running, things keep happening. This was before AI was even a thing, purely because of systems[^4].

### From Screen Recordings to SOPs with AI

The course can show how to go from recording a screen with Loom to creating a process document. You do not need an assistant for this anymore. AI can create the standard operating procedures (SOPs) from recordings. The participants will have to take time to record videos and document their processes, but that is it. The assistant part can be replaced with a markdown document and an agent that processes the recordings[^4].

### Knowledge Trapped in Team Members' Heads

One of the book's themes is being aware of whether knowledge is trapped in the heads of team members. This is a real problem in the DataTalks.Club operation too. The community manager's work is always documented - she follows the process. But the contact manager is different. She does a lot of useful work, and it is not always clear exactly how she does it. This knowledge needs to be documented so that if she is absent, someone else can step in. It is important to realize where these shortcomings are and do something about them. This makes a good topic for the course[^5].

### Using Podcasts to Create Systems

The book describes using a podcast as a way to create systems. The author would invite experts on different topics and then turn these conversations into playbooks and documents. This is something to explore as well, but it requires being more organized about how questions are asked. Worth checking how the Systemology podcast structures its interviews[^6].

## Credibility and Current Status

The Systemology book is great. After finishing it, the more this course idea develops, the more it seems worth focusing first on something closer to existing expertise[^7].

Sergey Sedler from AI Shipping Labs put it directly: if he wanted to learn business automation, he would go to someone with business experience. Alexey's credibility is in engineering, not in business. When offered to take the course for free, Sergey declined. He does not see value in it because there is no association with Alexey as a successful business person. He sees value in what Alexey is already known for: engineering[^7].

The course is already listed on Maven but will not be actively promoted. The plan is to look for people from the existing audience who have this problem. Reaching random businesses is not realistic yet - the reputation is not sufficient for them to trust someone whose background is engineering. If a few people sign up and the course works for them, that will be worth continuing. Building this credibility takes a lot of time. That does not mean stopping - the topic is genuinely interesting, worth reading and listening to books about. For now, this is something to work on in parallel, not the main focus[^7].
