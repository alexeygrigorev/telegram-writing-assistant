# Research Guide — Finding the Article's Angle

The goal of research is to find **3-5 article angles** worth pitching. A strong angle gives a single article one arguable thesis, a timely hook, a practical payoff, and real sources to cite.

## Topic-quality heuristics

An angle is strong when it passes all four:

1. **It has one arguable thesis.** You can state it in a sentence and a thoughtful designer could disagree.
2. **It's timely.** Something concrete in the last 30-60 days makes the conversation feel urgent (a launch, a viral post, fresh research, a public argument).
3. **It has a practical payoff.** You can hand the reader a method, framework, checklist, or set of patterns. Even essay-style pieces need a takeaway.
4. **It's citable.** Real sources exist for the stats and claims. If the angle relies on numbers you can't source, it's weak.

An angle is weak when:

- It's a trend roundup with no argument.
- It's evergreen with no current hook.
- The payoff is vague ("be more thoughtful").
- The claims can't be sourced.

## Angle-presentation format

Bring 3-5 angles back to Vadym, each like this:

```markdown
### Angle {N}: {Working title direction}

**Thesis (1 sentence):** {The argument the article makes.}

**Best template:** {Practical Workflow / Strategic Essay + Framework / Psychology Explainer / Product Teardown}

**Why now:** {The concrete recent event/post/research that makes it timely. Link 1-2 sources.}

**Practical payoff:** {The checklist, framework, or patterns the reader walks away with.}

**Risk / tradeoff:** {What's hard about writing it, or what nuance might get lost.}
```

Then ask Vadym to pick one (or adjust). Once he picks, research that angle deeper, capturing every URL you'll cite, before moving to the outline.

## A note on citations

Citation density is a signature of these articles. Almost every stat, product, person, event, and named principle gets an inline link. Capture URLs *as you research*, never reconstruct them later. If you can't find a real source for a claim during drafting, drop the claim or flag it `[VERIFY: needs source]`. Never invent a link or a statistic.
